# The Complete Wooden House Maintenance Guide

A wooden house is an investment that rewards careful stewardship. Unlike concrete or brick, timber is a living material that responds to its environment — and with the right care, it will reward you with a home that improves with age.

## Annual Inspection Checklist

Every spring, before the main maintenance season, walk around your house and check:

- **Exterior cladding** — look for cracks, splits, or areas where the paint or stain has peeled
- **Window and door frames** — check the sealant around all openings for gaps or deterioration
- **Roof** — inspect for missing or damaged shingles, and check the flashing around chimneys and skylights
- **Foundation** — ensure no soil or vegetation is in contact with the timber structure
- **Gutters and downpipes** — clear any blockages and check for leaks

## Exterior Painting and Staining

The exterior finish is your house's primary defence against moisture. Most wooden houses need repainting or re-staining every **5–8 years**, depending on climate and exposure.

**Signs it's time to repaint:**
- Paint is peeling, cracking, or chalking
- Bare wood is visible in any area
- Water is no longer beading on the surface

**Our recommended approach:**
1. Clean the surface thoroughly with a pressure washer
2. Allow to dry completely (at least 48 hours)
3. Sand any rough areas and spot-prime bare wood
4. Apply two coats of high-quality exterior wood paint or stain

## Dealing with Moisture

Moisture is the primary enemy of timber. The key is prevention:

- **Ensure good ventilation** under the floor and in the roof space
- **Keep gutters clear** so water doesn't overflow onto the walls
- **Maintain a 150mm gap** between the ground and any timber
- **Check internal humidity** — aim for 40–60% relative humidity year-round

If you find any areas of dampness or discolouration, address them immediately. Small problems become large ones quickly when moisture is involved.

## Treating Timber Naturally

For those who prefer a natural approach, linseed oil is an excellent traditional treatment for exterior timber. Applied annually, it penetrates the wood and provides good moisture resistance while allowing the timber to breathe.

Raw linseed oil takes a long time to dry — we recommend boiled linseed oil for exterior use, applied in thin coats on a warm, dry day.

## When to Call a Professional

Some maintenance tasks are best left to professionals:

- **Structural repairs** — any work involving load-bearing elements
- **Roof replacement** — working at height requires proper equipment and training
- **Significant rot treatment** — if rot has penetrated more than 20mm, professional assessment is needed
- **Insulation upgrades** — improving thermal performance requires careful detailing to avoid condensation problems
